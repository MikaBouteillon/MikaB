01

The concept

MiniMundo is not a traditional indoor playground. It is a miniature city of small streets and themed buildings, a shopfront on every façade, that children move through freely, discovering trades, shops and everyday situations. Positioning is premium: between roleplay, interactive play park and immersive experience.

Streets, not modules

Façades and interiors are the experience. Realistic, detail-rich architecture reading as a continuous street.

Four kinds of play

Roleplay, interactive & educational games, physical soft-play built into the buildings, and free unstructured play.

Detail-rich art direction

Shopfronts, signage, mouldings, shutters, planters, lighting, street furniture and greenery, all colourful, playful and warm.

Not a copy

The goal is a freer, more accessible experience, not a replication of an existing concept.

Design brief

Show us thinking around circulation, visual continuity between façades (heights, tones, rhythm) and a hierarchy of spaces: landmark buildings, secondary lanes, a central square where relevant.

02

The action zone

Alongside the streets of the miniature city we want one big action zone: a different world, louder and more physical, built for the wow effect and for high capacity. It is what lets us welcome a lot of children at once, and it gives visitors somewhere to burn energy between the roleplay spaces.

Illuminated slides

Slides that light up. Colour, movement and light are what pull children in from across the room.

Construction & excavation

A dig site with ride-on and hand-operated diggers. Children move real material, not just look at machines.

Ball pit park

A large ball pool with games built into it: cannons, targets, tunnels, climbing. Not an empty pool of balls.

Ninja course

A short obstacle course of the kind children already know: hanging steps, ropes, balance beams, a soft landing.

Your call

These four can sit together in one big zone or be split across the floor. Tell us what you think works best, and how many children each element handles at the same time.

04

What these images mean.

The images in the next section are not the design we want. They show the level of detail we want. Do not copy them. They are style references, not photos of existing builds. Bring us your own design, at this level of detail or better.

What matters most to us

The detail of the façades. The quality of the toys and props children handle. And an experience where a child can play in many different ways. These three points decide how we judge your work, together with price and lead time.

Façade detail

Shopfronts, illuminated signage, mouldings, shutters, planters. This is our first priority.

Toys & props

Everything a child picks up and handles. Counters, shelving, tools, costumes, equipment. Toy quality is judged as closely as the buildings.

Many ways to play

One space should offer several things to do. A child who returns should find something new.

Materials & light

Real, durable textures in timber, brick, metal or equivalent. Not bare plastic. Backlit signage and a lighting mood per space.

06

Technical specifications

What the build has to deliver architecturally, in materials, and in safety. Material choice is open; the outcome is not.

Façade detail

  • Open or semi-open shopfronts allowing a view into each space
  • Doors and windows as genuine architectural openings, not cut-outs
  • Distinct signage per building, ideally backlit or illuminated
  • Mouldings, shutters, planters, street furniture: lampposts, benches, street signage
  • Varied materials and textures from one building to the next

Activity types

  • Roleplay, re-enacting everyday situations
  • Interactive & educational games encouraging manipulation and experimentation
  • Physical & soft-play built directly into the buildings and façades
  • Free play, with no imposed route
  • The action zone of section 02: slides, dig site, ball pit and ninja course, sized for high capacity
  • Overall coherence: the venue must read as a street, not a collection of standalone modules

Materials & maintenance

  • Any solution, whether timber, composite, metal or other, that achieves the look, feel and durability of section 05
  • Durable and suited to intensive daily public use in a supervised children's venue (ERP)
  • Easy to clean and maintain daily
  • Finishes consistent with the reference gallery
  • Specify the exact materials proposed for each module

Child safety

  • Intensive daily use by young children, so reflect this in material and construction choices from the outset
  • Soft, impact-absorbing play surfaces, walls and structural edges within children's reach
  • Rounded corners and edges throughout; no sharp elements at child height
  • Non-slip, easy-to-disinfect flooring in all play areas
  • All materials non-toxic and compliant with children's product safety standards

Regulatory (ERP)

  • Design compatible with French accessibility, fire safety and smoke extraction requirements
  • Constraints confirmed jointly with the project architect during detailed costing
  • You are not expected to fully anticipate them at this stage, but do indicate your ability to comply

Certification

  • Play equipment and furniture must meet safety standards applicable in the country of installation: France / European Union
  • Detail on product certifications is requested in section 07
